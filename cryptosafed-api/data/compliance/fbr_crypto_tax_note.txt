Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) Guidance Note on Cryptocurrency and Digital Assets Taxation

Pakistan's Federal Board of Revenue treats income and gains arising from the sale, exchange, or disposal of cryptocurrencies and other digital assets as taxable under existing income tax law, even though cryptocurrencies are not recognized as legal tender in Pakistan. Taxpayers holding or trading digital assets are expected to declare related income or capital gains in their annual tax returns.

Profits from crypto trading may be treated as business income or capital gains depending on the frequency and nature of the taxpayer's activity. Failure to declare crypto-related income can result in penalties, audits, or other enforcement action under applicable tax laws.

FBR has indicated that as digital asset regulation evolves, more specific reporting requirements and tax treatment for virtual assets may be introduced. Taxpayers dealing in cryptocurrency are advised to maintain clear transaction records and consult a qualified tax professional regarding their specific obligations.
